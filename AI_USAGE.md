# AI Usage Log

I used Claude and Codex for research, drafting, implementation and verification support. My role in the recorded exchanges included defining the scope and game rules, choosing lifecycle and privacy requirements, challenging proposals and requesting corrections. The entries below connect those decisions to what changed, including work implemented by AI and checks that remained pending.

Prompts are summarized rather than presented as verbatim transcripts. Dates, branch states, test results and acceptance notes describe the recorded checkpoint; later changes do not retroactively change that history. Disposition records the treatment of the assistance at that checkpoint, separately from my review of these explanations.

## Recorded decisions and class principles

- **Planning and requirements:** I narrowed v1, deferred mashups and supplied concrete player limits, round counts, answer windows and retention choices. This follows the class distinction between scope, functional requirements and measurable constraints; it gave the implementation explicit rules to work toward rather than an open-ended feature list.
- **Feasibility before full implementation:** I kept the provider PoC separate from the application and challenged the assumption that a blocked personal-history endpoint meant the whole provider was unusable. The experiment separated listening input, catalog lookup and preview playback, giving the architecture a more precise basis.
- **Reviewing business rules:** I required owners to play under the same scoring rules, challenged a listener formula that rewarded selecting everyone and introduced Nobody rounds. The worked examples and subsequent scoring tests made those decisions checkable.
- **Single responsibility and modularity:** I requested code organization and cleanup against the course principles. The resulting structure separates Rooms membership/lifecycle from Game rounds/scoring, with persistence behind repositories, HTTP workflows in application commands and frontend transport, audio and presentation in separate modules. These are logical boundaries within the assignment's single-process application.
- **Dependency inversion and test boundaries:** The class-based cleanup request resulted in explicit collaborators and isolated rule tests: frontend `createRuntime` receives transport/audio/rendering dependencies, and backend rule tests use repository doubles. This makes behavior testable without requiring a live provider or browser for every business-rule check.
- **Iteration and acceptance evidence:** I revised requirements through successive checkpoints, reported cross-device failures and requested fixes, privacy enforcement and teacher setup improvements. The log distinguishes automated results, user-confirmed device behavior and outstanding checks, so a passing test suite is not presented as proof of every playback or setup scenario.

Course connections: *SDLC_phases* slides, pp. 5, 10–15, 19 and 23 (planning, requirements, design, testing, PoC and iteration); *solid_code_smells* slides, pp. 3, 6–7 and 15 (SOLID, separation of responsibilities and dependency injection). These connections describe recorded decisions and concrete implementation boundaries; they do not assert that every SOLID principle applies to every module.
